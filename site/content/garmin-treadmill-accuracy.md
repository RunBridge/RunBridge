Your watch has to estimate indoor running speed when GPS cannot measure forward movement. That estimate can be useful at a steady pace, but it may disagree with the treadmill—especially when pace, incline, arm motion or stride changes.

## Why indoor pace is difficult for a watch

Outdoors, GPS supplies distance and speed. Indoors, a watch commonly estimates them from wrist motion and a learned relationship between cadence, stride and pace. Holding the handrails, changing form, doing short intervals or wearing the watch on a less active arm can all change the signals available to that estimate.

The treadmill is measuring a different thing: its own belt or motor movement. Its displayed speed is not automatically a guarantee of physical belt accuracy, but it is the pace reference most runners use to control an indoor workout.

## What Garmin treadmill calibration changes

Garmin's treadmill calibration can scale the recorded distance after a run. It is most useful when the watch's error is reasonably consistent at similar paces. Follow the calibration instructions for your watch and use a long enough run for the watch to offer calibration.

Calibration cannot make every future pace change immediately visible. A single scale factor may not describe different walking, easy-running and interval strides, and post-run calibration does not fix misleading live pace during the workout.

## Why intervals expose the mismatch

Short intervals ask the measurement system to react quickly. A wrist estimate may lag as your stride changes, and recovery sections can have a different relationship between wrist motion and belt speed. That matters when a Garmin workout uses live pace to show whether you are inside a target range.

## Ways to improve treadmill data

1. **Use wrist estimation and calibrate.** This costs nothing and may be sufficient for steady runs.
2. **Use a running foot pod.** A sensor on the runner can provide pace and distance, but it measures the runner rather than reading the treadmill.
3. **Use a belt or treadmill sensor.** These measure treadmill movement independently, with their own setup and calibration considerations.
4. **Use the treadmill's FTMS data.** On a compatible treadmill, RunBridge receives the speed and distance the treadmill reports and presents that data to Garmin as a familiar running sensor.

RunBridge does not recalibrate the treadmill itself. It aligns Garmin with the treadmill-reported workout data rather than promising that the treadmill's physical belt speed is perfect.
